Technical model

How a team keeps its working theory available.

Augmented Workflow gives intent, reasoning, correction, and verification distinct places to live. Those artifacts help contributors rebuild a useful understanding of a system as it changes.

Read the overview of software continuity →

What the artifacts can carry.

A system's working theory is exercised by people who understand its problem, history, and consequences. Documents and code can support that work. They cannot settle every question or absorb accountability on their own.

What the workflow preserves

Evidence that gives a future contributor a better starting point for interpretation and judgment.

What remains with participants

Meaning, tradeoffs, and responsibility. A team still has to decide whether an old decision applies, whether a requirement is complete, and whether a change protects what matters.

Evidence has different jobs.

Putting every kind of context into one undifferentiated note makes it harder to evaluate. The model separates what is current, what was decided, what was learned, and what was checked.

Intent

Living specs

Describe expected behavior and keep changes in product intent visible to the people implementing and reviewing them.

Reasoning

Decisions

Record why a consequential choice made sense at the time, including the context a later decision may revisit.

Correction

Learnings

Preserve a validated lesson when the same misunderstanding would otherwise return in a later task or session.

Confidence

Verification records

Show what was tested, reviewed, or inspected so confidence can be understood and challenged in context.

Evidence is renewed through the work.

The shape is a loop because each meaningful change can reveal something that needs to be clarified, corrected, or preserved for the next one.

01

Frame

Bring current intent and relevant history into view before choosing an approach.

02

Change

Make the smallest justified change and record assumptions that affect its meaning.

03

Check

Test behavior and inspect the reasoning, boundaries, and consequences of the work.

04

Capture

Update living intent, preserve a decision, or retain a learning when the work earned one.

05

Carry forward

Leave the next contributor enough evidence to begin with a clearer theory of the system.

Test the intended experience before building.

When the experience is uncertain, aw-brainstorm makes the agent's interpretation tangible enough for people to question:

  1. Interpret intent. Explain what people should understand, accomplish, and feel, and which qualities of any references support that experience.
  2. Suggest a prototype. Describe what a small disposable example would clarify.
  3. Wait for agreement. Create the suggested prototype only after agreement. An explicit request to create one already authorizes its stated scope.
  4. Gather a human reaction. Inspect the experience together. Correct behavior alone cannot establish whether it feels right; unresolved fit stays visible.
  5. Preserve the learning. Update living intent with what was learned. The prototype can be discarded once it has served its purpose.

Routine changes with clear intent need no prototype. If someone declines a suggestion, continue authorized work with the uncertainty explicit.

What a deterministic layer can establish.

A gate can show that a process left evidence. It cannot determine whether the work was done well. That judgment remains with the people who understand the change and its consequences.

Evidence a gate can collect

  • Whether required artifacts are present and linked
  • Whether a stated behavior has verification coverage
  • Whether review and delivery checkpoints were recorded
  • Whether the evidence is fresh enough for the stated policy

Questions people still answer

  • Whether the evidence is relevant to this decision
  • Which tradeoff is appropriate in the current situation
  • Whether a result advances the product's purpose
  • Whether the experience helps people understand, act, and feel as intended
  • What should become durable knowledge for the team

Consider a parent asking children to wash their hands before dinner. The parent cannot watch every moment at the sink. They can look for evidence: enough time in the bathroom, running water, and clean hands. aw-gate.js works the same way. It records observable facts about the workflow so a team can see whether the expected process took place and focus its judgment where it matters.

Agent participation

Give agents a basis for responsible work.

Agents are useful when their work can be connected to current intent, prior reasoning, and observable checks. That connection gives reviewers something concrete to inspect and gives an agent a way to expose what it could not determine.

  • Traverse evidence Read the relevant specification, decision history, standards, and prior learnings before proposing a change.
  • Make uncertainty visible State assumptions and unresolved questions where the available evidence does not support a confident conclusion.
  • Return inspectable work Leave a clear account of changes, checks, decisions, and remaining risks for the accountable people who review it.
A durable practice

Keep the next explanation close to the next change.

Augmented Workflow is designed for teams that want software to remain explainable as contributors, requirements, and tools evolve.
